\begin{abstract}
The Indonesian clitic \id{-nya} is one of the highest-frequency morphological items in the language and serves at least four distinct functions --- possessive, definite, anaphoric, and nominalising --- yet standard Indonesian information-retrieval pipelines treat it as a generic suffix to be stripped. We test whether this preprocessing default helps or harms modern retrieval effectiveness through a controlled four-condition study on the Indonesian split of MIRACL: (i)~Keep, (ii)~Naive regex strip, (iii)~dictionary-aware Sastrawi clitic strip, and (iv)~Sentinel masking. We evaluate both sparse (BM25) and dense (BGE-m3) retrievers and report nDCG@10 and MRR@100 across all conditions, with a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank protocol and a Bonferroni-corrected pairwise post-hoc. \todo{Insert headline result once experiments complete.} Our findings inform the preprocessing decisions made by every Indonesian retrieval system that currently inherits English-centric defaults and quantify, for the first time, the harm of the naive \id{-nya} regex that is widely distributed in practitioner code.
\end{abstract}
