\begin{abstract}
Late Chunking \citep{gunther2024latechunking} reports double-digit gains on long-context retrieval by pooling chunk embeddings over a full-document forward pass, but its mechanism is unspecified: it is unclear whether the gains come from cross-sentence anaphora resolution or from a bag-of-document smoothing effect. Indonesian is an exceptionally clean probe of this question because (i)~it is a pro-drop language in which subjects are routinely null and reference is carried by the polysemous clitic \id{-nya}, and (ii)~its sentences can be shuffled to destroy anaphora chains while preserving bag-of-words cohesion. We run a controlled study on the Indonesian split of MIRACL \citep{zhang2023miracl} comparing Naive (per-chunk) and Late (full-document) chunking under BGE-m3 \citep{chen2024bgem3}, stratified by anaphora type (\id{-nya} clitic, overt pronoun, zero-anaphora, none) and complemented by a sentence-shuffle ablation that severs sequential cues without changing token content. We address five pre-registered questions: does Late actually resolve cross-sentence anaphora (RQ1), are gains heterogeneous by anaphora type (RQ2), do they transfer across native vs.\ translated id-Wiki (RQ3), do they survive cross-encoder reranking (RQ4), and how do they interact with chunk size (RQ5). \todo{Insert headline result once experiments complete.} The study provides the first mechanistic test of Late Chunking on a pro-drop, morphologically rich language, and bounds when long-context contextualization is and is not the right intervention for Indonesian information retrieval.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} information retrieval, late chunking, anaphora resolution, Indonesian, pro-drop, dense retrieval, MIRACL.
