\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Four bodies of work are directly relevant: the linguistics and computational morphology of Indonesian \id{-nya}; Indonesian information retrieval and stemming; anaphora and coreference resolution applied to retrieval; and the general question of preprocessing for transformer-based retrievers.

\subsection{Indonesian morphology and the clitic \id{-nya}}

\id{-nya} is descriptively well-documented in the linguistics literature, where it is consistently analysed as polysemous across at least four functions: possessive third-person singular, definite marker, anaphoric pronoun, and nominaliser~\cite{denistia2021morphology}. Computational morphology tools that handle Indonesian cliticisation include MorphInd~\cite{morphind_larasati} and the recent IndoMorph engine~\cite{indomorph2025}. Larasati's earlier treatment of Indonesian clitics in the context of machine translation~\cite{larasati2012clitics} provides one of the few public datasets distinguishing clitic from non-clitic occurrences of the \id{-nya} surface form. The most directly relevant computational study of the clitic comes from Malay rather than Indonesian: a recent Malay-clitic classification study~\cite{malay_nya_2021} classifies possessive \id{nya} into pleonastic, human-referring, and non-human-referring uses with accuracies of 88\%, 94\%, and 63\% respectively, but stops at classification and does not connect to retrieval.

\subsection{Indonesian information retrieval and stemming}

The canonical study of Indonesian stemming for IR is the study by Tala~\cite{tala2003stemming}, which evaluates several stemming algorithms on a TREC-style Indonesian collection using pre-neural vector-space methods. The result --- that stemming generally helps Indonesian IR --- has been widely cited but has not, to our knowledge, been re-validated against modern dense retrieval. Subsequent work compares stemming algorithms (Sastrawi~\cite{sastrawi}, Nazief--Adriani~\cite{nazief1996indonesian}, Porter variants) on \emph{stemming accuracy} rather than retrieval effectiveness; Sastrawi consistently reports the highest accuracy at approximately 95.2\%. Indonesian language models including IndoBERT~\cite{wilie2020indonlu} and IndoLEM~\cite{koto2020indolem} have established benchmarks for Indonesian NLU, and the NusaCrowd consortium~\cite{cahyawijaya2023nusacrowd} aggregates open Indonesian NLP resources. The Indonesian split of MIRACL~\cite{zhang2023miracl} provides a high-quality, native-annotated retrieval benchmark, and Mr.\ TyDi~\cite{zhang2021mrtydi} additionally provides an Indonesian split designed for dense-retrieval evaluation. None of these resources isolates clitic preprocessing as an experimental variable.

\subsection{Anaphora, coreference, and retrieval}

Mitkov~\cite{mitkov2002anaphora} demonstrated in the pre-neural era that anaphora resolution improves both retrieval and question answering for English, and a recent ACL Student Research Workshop paper by Jang et al.~\cite{jang2025coref} extends the finding to modern retrieval-augmented generation, showing that explicit coreference resolution measurably improves both retrieval and downstream generation for English RAG. For Indonesian specifically, Auliarachman and Purwarianti~\cite{auliarachman2020indocoref} present a CNN-based mention-pair coreference resolver achieving an F1 of approximately 76\% with gold-singleton classification, and Saputri et al.~\cite{saputri2021indonesian} present a multi-pass sieve resolver; neither system is connected to retrieval evaluation. The intersection of \id{-nya}-aware preprocessing and modern Indonesian dense retrieval --- the focus of the present study --- is, after a deliberate scoping search, the gap that this paper fills.

\subsection{Preprocessing for transformer-based models}

A recent comparative survey by Camacho-Collados et al.~\cite{camacho2023preprocessing} reports that classical preprocessing techniques (stemming, stopword removal, lowercasing) often do not benefit transformer-based classifiers in English-centric tasks. The generality of that finding to morphologically distinctive low-resource languages, and specifically to retrieval rather than classification, is open. Our results provide one data point.
