\begin{description}
    \item[\textbf{H1} (mechanism).] Late Chunking's nDCG@10 gain over Naive on anaphora-bearing queries shrinks by at least 50\% under sentence-shuffle, while its gain on lexical-overlap (no-anaphora) queries is shuffle-invariant. \textit{Test:} paired bootstrap on per-query $\Delta$nDCG@10 with method $\times$ shuffle interaction (one-tailed, $\alpha = 0.05$).
    \item[\textbf{H2} (anaphora stratification).] Late Chunking's per-query gain is ordered \id{-nya}~clitic $>$ overt pronoun $>$ zero-anaphora $>$ no-anaphora. \textit{Test:} stratified analysis with Holm-corrected pairwise Wilcoxon signed-rank across the four strata.
    \item[\textbf{H3} (provenance).] Translated id-Wiki passages exhibit larger Late-over-Naive gains than native id-Wiki passages, because translationese over-realizes the pronouns the encoder binds through. \textit{Test:} provenance $\times$ method ANOVA on per-document $\Delta$nDCG@10, $\alpha = 0.05$.
    \item[\textbf{H4} (reranker absorption).] When BGE-reranker-v2-m3 reranks the top-100 candidates, the Late--Naive gain at nDCG@10 drops to at most 25\% of its first-stage magnitude. \textit{Test:} two-stage paired bootstrap with pre/post-rerank delta, one-tailed.
    \item[\textbf{H5} (chunk-size interaction).] Late's relative gain at chunk size 256 is larger than at 512. \textit{Test:} method $\times$ size interaction term in a linear mixed model, $\alpha = 0.05$.
\end{description}
