\begin{abstract}
The Indonesian clitic \id{-nya} is frequent and polysemous, marking possessive, definite, anaphoric, and nominalising functions, yet Indonesian retrieval pipelines often treat it as a generic suffix. We test five preprocessing strategies on MIRACL-id: Keep, Naive regex strip, dictionary-aware Sastrawi clitic strip, Sentinel masking, and Rule-based anaphora resolution. We evaluate BM25 and BGE-m3 with paired per-query tests. BM25 shows a statistically significant omnibus preprocessing effect, but the top strategies differ by less than 0.001 nDCG@10; BGE-m3 is effectively insensitive to the tested transformations, and the rule-based resolver does not improve retrieval. The practical recommendation is conservative: preserve raw text for dense models, and use dictionary-guarded clitic handling rather than naive regex stripping when suffix handling is required.
\end{abstract}
