\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study isolates Indonesian \id{-nya} preprocessing as an experimental variable in MIRACL-id retrieval and finds that its aggregate effect is small. BM25 shows a statistically significant omnibus difference across the five strategies, but the strongest strategies are separated by less than 0.001 nDCG@10; BGE-m3 is even more stable, with all strategies clustered within roughly 0.002 nDCG@10. The practical recommendation is therefore conservative: preserve raw text by default for dense retrieval, and prefer dictionary-guarded Sastrawi-style clitic handling over naive regex stripping when a sparse or stemming-based Indonesian pipeline requires suffix handling.

The in-house rule-based anaphora resolver does not improve retrieval under the tested configuration. That result should be interpreted narrowly: a cheap deterministic resolver introduces more ranking noise than useful signal here, but it does not rule out gains from stronger Indonesian coreference models, oracle diagnostics, or domain-specific anaphora-aware retrieval. Future work should therefore separate resolver quality from retrieval architecture more directly, ideally across multiple Indonesian benchmarks and with qualitative inspection of high-sensitivity queries.

The released artifacts include the preprocessing pipeline, retrieval and evaluation scripts, per-query metric CSVs for all ten retriever-by-strategy conditions, statistical-analysis outputs, and integrity-check reports. Together, these artifacts make the negative result reproducible and give Indonesian IR practitioners a concrete baseline for deciding whether \id{-nya}-specific preprocessing is worth the added complexity.
