\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Four bodies of work are directly relevant to this study: long-context and document-aware dense retrieval, Indonesian morphology with a focus on the clitic \id{-nya}, anaphora resolution in retrieval and question answering, and the broader question of preprocessing for transformer-based models.

\subsection{Long-context and document-aware dense retrieval}

Conventional dense retrieval encodes each chunk in isolation. \citet{karpukhin2020dpr} established the dual-encoder design that underlies most modern systems; \citet{reimers2019sbert} extended sentence-level embeddings to ranking tasks via Siamese training. With the advent of long-context encoders such as Longformer \citep{beltagy2020longformer} and the Jina embeddings family \citep{mohr2023jina}, which uses ALiBi \citep{press2022alibi} to handle inputs of up to 8{,}192 tokens, an alternative emerged. \citet{gunther2024latechunking} introduce \emph{Late Chunking}: the encoder processes the full document in a single forward pass, and chunk vectors are obtained by mean-pooling token embeddings within each chunk's span post hoc. The reported result is a 5--24\% relative improvement in nDCG on long-context retrieval benchmarks. The paper, however, does not localise the gain to any specific linguistic phenomenon. \citet{wang2024dapr} provide complementary evidence that 53.5\% of retrieval failures on a document-aware benchmark stem from missing document context. Neither study disentangles the contributions of (i)~cross-sentence anaphora resolution, (ii)~document-wide token co-occurrence, and (iii)~smoothing of per-chunk embeddings toward a document-level mean. The present work targets that disentanglement, using sentence-shuffle and anaphora stratification as the experimental tools.

\subsection{Indonesian morphology and the clitic \id{-nya}}

Indonesian \id{-nya} is consistently analysed in the linguistics literature as polysemous across at least four functions: third-person singular possessive, definite marker, anaphoric pronoun, and verbal nominaliser \citep{denistia2021morphology}. Computational morphology resources that handle Indonesian cliticisation include MorphInd \citep{morphind_larasati} and the recent IndoMorph engine \citep{indomorph2025}. The most directly relevant computational study of the clitic comes from Malay rather than Indonesian: \citet{malay_nya_2021} classify possessive \id{nya} into pleonastic, human-referring, and non-human-referring uses with accuracies of 88\%, 94\%, and 63\% respectively. That work stops at classification and does not connect to retrieval. Indonesian benchmarks for general NLU include IndoNLU \citep{wilie2020indonlu} and IndoLEM \citep{koto2020indolem}, and the NusaCrowd consortium \citep{cahyawijaya2023nusacrowd} aggregates resources across the Indonesian languages. None of these isolates anaphoric reference structure as a controlled retrieval variable.

\subsection{Anaphora resolution applied to retrieval}

\citet{mitkov2002anaphora} showed in the pre-neural era that anaphora resolution improves both retrieval and question answering for English. For Indonesian specifically, \citet{saputri2021indonesian} present a multi-pass sieve coreference resolver but do not connect it to retrieval evaluation. End-to-end neural coreference systems originated in English \citep{lee2017end2end} and have inspired multilingual extensions, but Indonesian coreference remains underserved. No prior study, to our knowledge, evaluates whether Late Chunking or any other long-context encoder substitutes for explicit anaphora resolution in Indonesian retrieval. That gap motivates the present work.

\subsection{Preprocessing for transformer-based retrievers}

The general question of whether classical preprocessing (stemming, stopword removal, lowercasing) still helps transformer-based models has received empirical attention. \citet{camacho2023preprocessing} report that, for English text classification, the answer is generally no. The most authoritative empirical study on Indonesian-specific preprocessing for retrieval, \citet{tala2003stemming}, predates BERT and dense retrieval entirely; subsequent comparisons of Sastrawi \citep{sastrawi} and Nazief--Adriani \citep{nazief1996indonesian} evaluate stemming accuracy rather than retrieval effectiveness. The multilingual benchmarks MIRACL \citep{zhang2023miracl} and Mr.\ TyDi \citep{zhang2021mrtydi} provide native-annotated Indonesian splits; the dense backbones BGE-m3 \citep{chen2024bgem3} and multilingual E5 \citep{wang2024multilingual_e5} dominate current state of the art. The present study does not propose a new preprocessing or encoder; it asks whether the long-context contextualization promised by Late Chunking does the work that explicit anaphora preprocessing would otherwise need to do, on a language where that question has structural bite.

\subsection{Linguistic-typology motivations for the design}

This study is also informed by methodological work on statistical significance in NLP \citep{dror2018hitchhiker}, which underlies the paired-bootstrap and Wilcoxon protocols specified in \secref{sec:method}, and by surveys of linguistic resource inequity \citep{joshi2020state} that frame the broader contribution of evaluating long-context methods on a non-English typology.
