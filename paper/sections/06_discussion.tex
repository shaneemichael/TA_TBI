\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Interpretation}

The main result is conservative: \id{-nya} preprocessing is not a large source of retrieval-effectiveness variation on MIRACL-id dev. BM25 shows a significant omnibus difference, but the strongest strategies differ by less than 0.001 nDCG@10. BGE-m3 is even less sensitive, with all strategies clustered within roughly 0.002 nDCG@10. Thus, Indonesian retrieval systems should avoid linguistically elaborate rewriting unless a task-specific validation set shows a real gain.

The expected harm from naive stripping also does not appear at benchmark scale. The regex corrupts tokens such as \id{punya}, \id{tanya}, and proper names, but those errors are not often decisive for ranking gold passages. If suffix handling is needed for a sparse or stemming-based pipeline, Sastrawi clitic with dictionary guarding is still preferable because it avoids known false positives with no observed effectiveness cost.

The in-house Rule-resolved strategy does not improve retrieval. This is not evidence that Indonesian anaphora resolution is useless; it shows that this cheap surface resolver is not accurate enough to help. The BGE-m3 Sentinel result should also not be over-read: \texttt{<NYA>} is an artificial OOV token outside BGE-m3 pretraining, so the Sentinel condition partly measures graceful handling of an unfamiliar marker rather than only the value of clitic-presence information. Stronger neural or oracle resolvers remain future work.

\subsection{Limitations and Validity}

Strategy~5 is a deterministic in-house resolver, not a neural coreference model or an oracle. Its known failure modes include choosing sentence-initial common nouns as antecedents and mishandling chained \id{-nya} references across sentences. The Sastrawi condition depends on Sastrawi's bundled root dictionary; rare names, technical terms, and lexicalised/adverbial forms such as \id{biasanya} or \id{karenanya} can still be handled imperfectly. All runs use MIRACL-id dev and one seed/configuration per retriever; FAISS HNSW construction can also vary under parallel execution, so we do not claim generalisation to legal, medical, social-media, or conversational Indonesian retrieval.

The sensitivity score is also heuristic, using surface counts and resolver-aligned anaphoric cues rather than gold coreference annotation. Its queried-entity indicator is conservative and can miss queries without explicit proper nouns. This makes H5 reproducible and scalable but limits how strongly stratified effects can be interpreted.
