\begin{abstract}
The Indonesian clitic \id{-nya} is one of the highest-frequency morphological items in the language and serves at least four distinct functions --- possessive, definite, anaphoric, and nominalising --- yet standard Indonesian information-retrieval pipelines treat it as a generic suffix to be stripped. We test whether this preprocessing default helps or harms modern retrieval effectiveness through a controlled five-condition study on the Indonesian split of MIRACL: (i)~Keep, (ii)~Naive regex strip, (iii)~dictionary-aware Sastrawi clitic strip, (iv)~Sentinel masking, and (v)~Rule-based anaphora resolution that rewrites anaphoric \id{-nya} occurrences with their resolved antecedent using surface-context heuristics and applies to all 1.4M passages at CPU-only cost. We evaluate both sparse (BM25) and dense (BGE-m3) retrievers and report nDCG@10 and MRR@100 across all conditions, with a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank protocol and Bonferroni-corrected pairwise post-hoc tests. BM25 shows a statistically significant omnibus preprocessing effect, but the top strategies differ by less than 0.001 nDCG@10; BGE-m3 is effectively insensitive to the tested transformations, and the rule-based resolver does not improve retrieval. Our findings support a conservative default for Indonesian retrieval: preserve raw text for dense models, and use dictionary-guarded clitic handling rather than naive regex stripping when suffix handling is required.
\end{abstract}
