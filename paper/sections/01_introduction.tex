\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Modern retrieval-augmented generation pipelines, including those now deployed in production for the Indonesian market, split documents into independent chunks before embedding them. Each chunk is encoded in isolation, indexed, and retrieved by similarity to the query. This default --- inherited largely from English-centric pipelines --- ignores a structural property of Indonesian that English does not share: Indonesian is a pro-drop language in which subjects are routinely null, and a single high-frequency clitic, \id{-nya}, carries definite, possessive, and anaphoric reference to entities mentioned several sentences earlier. The clitic appears in roughly 19.5\% of Indonesian suffix occurrences \citep{denistia2021morphology}. Consider three minimal examples that any naive-chunked pipeline silently degrades on:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Health.} ``\id{Pasien menderita hipertensi kronis. Dokter meresepkan amlodipin 10mg. Beliau menyarankan pasien mengonsumsinya setiap pagi.}'' The query \id{Kapan amlodipin diminum?} requires \id{-nya} in the third sentence to bind to \id{amlodipin} in the second.
    \item \textbf{Civic.} ``\id{Bantuan Pangan Non-Tunai cair Maret 2025. Penerima harus terdaftar di DTKS. Pencairannya dilakukan via Kartu Keluarga Sejahtera.}'' Here \id{-nya} reaches three sentences back to its antecedent.
    \item \textbf{News.} ``\id{Prabowo bertemu Xi Jinping di Beijing.}~$\emptyset$~\id{Membahas kerja sama ekonomi. Pertemuannya berlangsung dua jam.}'' Both the dropped subject in sentence~2 and the \id{-nya} in sentence~3 resolve to the entity named in sentence~1.
\end{itemize}

In English, equivalent references would be lexicalised as overt pronouns (\textit{it}, \textit{he}, \textit{the meeting}). An English chunk embedder sees those pronouns and can bind through them. An Indonesian chunk embedder, looking at the third sentence alone, sees neither the antecedent nor a lexical pointer to it.

\subsection{The mechanism question}

\citet{gunther2024latechunking} recently introduced \emph{Late Chunking}, in which a long-context encoder processes the entire document and per-chunk vectors are obtained by pooling token embeddings within each chunk's span \emph{after} the forward pass. Reported gains on long-context retrieval are large --- up to 24\% relative on selected benchmarks --- and the method is by now widely adopted. Yet the reason it works is unclear. \citet{wang2024dapr} report that 53.5\% of retrieval errors stem from missing document context, motivating the long-context approach; but the specific mechanism --- whether Late Chunking resolves anaphora in any linguistically meaningful sense or merely smooths chunk vectors towards a document-wide mean --- is unspecified. The mechanism question matters because the two stories make divergent predictions: if Late Chunking is genuinely anaphora-aware, it should help disproportionately when the query depends on an unresolved reference; if it is a regularizer over document-wide statistics, it should help uniformly regardless of reference structure.

\subsection{Indonesian as a probe}

Indonesian is, we argue, an unusually clean probe of this distinction. Its pro-drop typology means that anaphora is structurally salient: a sentence in isolation may contain no surface marker of its main referent at all. This makes the experiment a \emph{sentence-shuffle ablation} natural and informative. Shuffling the sentences of an Indonesian document destroys long-range anaphora chains while leaving the bag-of-words distribution of each chunk identical. If Late Chunking's gain on Indonesian survives sentence shuffle, the mechanism is not anaphora resolution. If the gain disappears, it is. To our knowledge no prior work has performed this test in any language.

Our contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item The first sentence-shuffle ablation of Late Chunking in any language, isolating sequential anaphora resolution from bag-of-document regularization (RQ1).
    \item An anaphora-stratified evaluation that decomposes Late Chunking's gain by reference type --- \id{-nya} clitic, overt pronoun, zero-anaphora, none --- on a manually annotated subset of MIRACL-id (RQ2).
    \item A native-vs-translated provenance contrast that uses translationese as a natural ablation of pro-drop, since translated Indonesian over-realizes pronouns (RQ3).
    \item A pipeline-level test of whether Late Chunking's first-stage gains survive cross-encoder reranking (RQ4), and a chunk-size interaction analysis grounded in Indonesian morphology (RQ5).
