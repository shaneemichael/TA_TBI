\begin{description}
    \item[\textbf{H1} (omnibus).] At least one of the five preprocessing strategies yields a significantly different per-query nDCG@10 distribution than the others, separately for each retriever architecture. \textit{Test:} Friedman's test, $\alpha = 0.05$.
    \item[\textbf{H2} (directional).] Naive regex \id{-nya} stripping yields significantly lower nDCG@10 than Keep on both retrievers, due to over-stripping of high-frequency non-clitic words such as \id{punya}, \id{tanya}, and \id{hanya}. \textit{Test:} one-tailed paired Wilcoxon signed-rank, $\alpha = 0.05$.
    \item[\textbf{H3} (architecture interaction).] The optimal preprocessing strategy among Keep, Sastrawi clitic, and Sentinel differs between BM25 and BGE-m3. \textit{Test:} comparison of best-performing strategy per retriever with bootstrap CI on the gain difference.
    \item[\textbf{H4} (oracle ceiling).] On the oracle-annotated subset, Strategy~5 (Oracle) significantly outperforms the best of Strategies~1--4 for both retrievers, establishing an upper bound on attainable improvement from anaphora-aware preprocessing. \textit{Test:} paired Wilcoxon, Bonferroni-corrected $\alpha = 0.025$.
    \item[\textbf{H5} (sensitivity stratification).] Effects of preprocessing strategy on retrieval effectiveness are larger on high-\id{-nya}-sensitivity queries than on low-sensitivity queries. \textit{Test:} mixed-effects model with strategy-by-stratum interaction.
\end{description}
